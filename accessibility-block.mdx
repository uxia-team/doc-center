The **Accessibility check** block audits your screens against WCAG 2.2 before you write code. You upload your screens and pick a target conformance level. Uxia then checks each screen criterion by criterion. It flags what passes, what fails, and what needs a human review, and it tags each issue as a likely design fix or code fix.

Because the audit runs on static screens rather than a live build, you catch accessibility problems at the design stage. That's where they're cheapest to fix, instead of late in QA or after launch.

## What it checks

- **WCAG 2.2 success criteria** at Level AA or AAA. The levels are cumulative, so an AA audit also covers Level A. See [WCAG conformance level](/wcag-conformance-level).
- **All four WCAG principles**: Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust. This includes contrast, text alternatives, touch target size, focus visibility, and labels.
- **Low-vision support**, with a [vision simulation](/vision-simulation) that shows each screen under color blindness, blurred vision, and field loss.
- **Design and code scope**. Some criteria can't be confirmed from a screenshot, such as keyboard behavior. Uxia marks these for review and tags them **Code** so your engineers can verify them.

## When to use it

**Pre-development design review.** Audit screens before handoff, so accessibility problems surface in design rather than in QA or after code ships.

**Splitting work between designers and engineers.** Every issue that needs work is tagged **Design** or **Code**. Both teams get a shared checklist and a clean way to divide the work.

**Compliance-driven reviews.** Check against a recognized standard: AA as the common legal baseline, or AAA when you aim for the highest coverage.

**Tracking progress over time.** Audit the updated screens after fixes and watch the Passed, Failed, and Review counts move in the right direction.

## Add the block

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the block picker">
    In the **Build** phase, open **Add a block**. Under **Audits**, choose **Accessibility check**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a conformance level">
    Keep **Recommended** (AA) or switch to **Advanced** (AAA). See [WCAG conformance level](/wcag-conformance-level).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your frames">
    Upload up to 5 screens as image screenshots (PNG, JPEG, WebP, or GIF). You can add each screen's live URL for extra code-level checks. See [Frames](/frame-upload).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Launch the audit">
    Click **Start recruiting**. If the test has no other blocks, you launch the audit straight from the Recruit summary. See [Run the audit](/frame-upload#run-the-audit).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Limits and credits

| | Free plan | Paid plans (Starter, Pro, Custom) |
|---|---|---|
| Accessibility check block | Not available | 1 per test |
| Frames | Not available | Up to 5 per test, 100 MB each |
| Cost | Not available | 10 credits per frame |

- On the Free plan, the block shows an **Upgrade** badge in the picker. Clicking it opens the **Upgrade to use accessibility** plans dialog.
- A test can hold only one accessibility check block. Once you add one, it's disabled in the picker. You also can't duplicate the block.
- The cost depends only on the number of frames. For example, an audit of 4 screens costs 40 credits, whatever participants the test has.
- In a test with [A/B variants](/ab-variants), the frame cost applies to each variant.

## Combine it with other blocks

An accessibility check can sit in the same test as task, survey, and card sorting blocks. It doesn't need participants:

- **AI participants.** The audit runs when you launch AI participants. Its frame credits are added to the launch total.
- **Human participants.** Participants who join through a [test link](/test-links) or the [human panel](/human-panel) skip the block. If you only recruit human participants, the audit doesn't run.
- **Accessibility only.** If the test has no other blocks, there's nothing to recruit. The Recruit phase tells you so, and you launch the audit from its summary.

Results appear in the **Analyze** phase as their own block, next to your other results.

<Note>
  Uxia helps you catch issues early, but it doesn't replace a formal legal accessibility audit, a specialist review, or a certification.
</Note>
