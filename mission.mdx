The **Task** field (formerly called *mission*) is the one concrete thing participants try to do. It's the most important field in a [Task block](/task-block): it defines the goal of the session and keeps every participant's feedback focused and actionable.

The task is required and takes up to 500 characters. The more specific and granular you make it, the more your results focus on the flow you want to analyze.

## Write it as a single concrete task

A good task describes one goal with a clear end state:

> _"Upgrade your plan from free to premium using a credit card."_

> _"Find and purchase two tickets for tonight's 9:00 PM showing."_

> _"Book a table for two at the first restaurant under Popular near you."_

## Tips for effective tasks

<Tip>
  **Start with an action verb.** Sign up, purchase, book, upload, send. Describe what to achieve, not what to evaluate: avoid verbs like "test", "explore", or "check".
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Name a single target.** If the screen could show several matching items, point to exactly one: "the first product under New arrivals", a named item, or a specific time slot. Avoid "any product".
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Lead with the goal, not the steps.** Write "Find a plan that fits your budget and sign up" rather than "Click Pricing, select Pro, then fill in your details." Step-by-step instructions train participants to follow directions instead of revealing where your design fails.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Don't tell participants how to give feedback.** AI participants already share thoughts, reactions, and feedback throughout every session, so phrases like "think out loud" or "share your first impression" are removed before the test runs.

  Instead of: "Take a quick look at the page, share your first impression, then buy a laptop and tell us what you are thinking."

  Write: "Purchase the first laptop in the laptops catalog."

  To ask for opinions, add a [Survey block](/survey-block) after the task.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  **Avoid leading language.** Don't name UI elements ("click the blue button"), imply a correct path ("find the easy way to…"), or signal what participants should feel ("notice how simple it is…"). Neutral wording is everything.
</Tip>

## One goal per task

Compound tasks produce muddled results. To test several flows, add a Task block for each one: up to 3 in one test on paid plans, and 1 on the Free plan. When a live website or Figma prototype task uses the same URL as an earlier one, you can turn on **Continue from where participants left the previous task**, so participants pick up where they stopped.

## Refine it with Tips

The **Task**, **Scenario**, and **Stop condition** fields each have a **Tips** button, the wand icon in the top-right corner of the field.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write a first version">
    Tips need at least 15 characters of text.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Tips">
    Uxia suggests rewritten versions and explains what each one improves. Use the arrows to browse them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply one">
    Click **Use this version** to replace your text, or close the suggestions to keep yours. To get new tips, edit the text first.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Warnings under the task

On live website and Figma prototype tasks, Uxia shows a warning under the **Task** field when your text mentions:

- a social login, which AI participants can't complete;
- a sign-up or registration flow, which must not require verification codes, validation emails, or 2FA;
- think-aloud or feedback instructions (in the task or the stop condition), which are removed before the test runs.

Click **Learn more** in the warning for a short explanation, or see [Flows AI participants can't complete](/unsupported-flows) for workarounds.

## Example

Suppose your business objective is:

> **_"Understand whether customers can successfully purchase a new mobile phone plan."_**

It may be tempting to test the entire journey with one task:

<Warning>
  **Too broad**

  _"Find a mobile plan that fits your needs, purchase it, and contact support if you have any questions."_
</Warning>

Although this sounds like a realistic customer journey, it combines several tasks:

- Finding the right plan
- Completing the purchase
- Asking for help

If participants struggle, it's much harder to tell which part of the experience caused the friction.

Instead, split the objective into separate Task blocks in the same test:

**Task block 1: Find a suitable plan**

<Check>
  _"Find a mobile plan that includes around 10 GB of monthly data and add it to your cart."_
</Check>

**Task block 2: Purchase the plan**

<Check>
  _"Purchase the mobile plan in your cart and add an additional product to your order."_
</Check>

Turn on **Continue from where participants left the previous task** here, so participants keep the plan they added to their cart.

**Task block 3: Get help**

<Check>
  _"Find how to contact customer support to ask a question about your subscription."_
</Check>

Each task measures one clear user goal. This makes the results easier to interpret and shows exactly where participants run into friction.
