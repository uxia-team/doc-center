A **Card sorting** block shows you how participants group and label your content. You give participants a set of cards (menu items, features, articles, products) and they sort them into categories that make sense to them. The result tells you whether your structure matches the way people think.

## When to use card sorting

- **Information architecture.** Decide how to organize the sections of a website, app, or help center.
- **Navigation labels.** Check whether your menu and category names mean to participants what they mean to you.
- **Content grouping.** Find out which items people expect to find together, such as settings, product categories, or dashboard widgets.

To check whether people can then *find* things in the new structure, follow up with a [Task block](/task-block).

## Open or closed sort

| | Closed | Open |
|---|---|---|
| Categories | You define them | Participants create and name their own |
| Best for | Testing a structure you already have | Discovering how people think about your content |
| What you learn | Where participants expect each card to live | Which groups and labels participants come up with |

Start with an **open** sort when you're exploring. Use a **closed** sort to validate the categories you've chosen.

There is no separate hybrid option. For something in between, use an **open** sort with starting categories: participants can use your categories and add their own.

## Set up a Card sorting block

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the block">
    In the **Build** phase, click **Add new block** (or **Add block** in the sidebar) and choose **Card sorting** under **Research**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write instructions (optional)">
    Use **Instructions** to tell participants what they're sorting and why, for example "Sort the menu items into the groups that make the most sense to you." Up to 1,000 characters. If you leave it empty, participants see a default instruction for the sort type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the sorting type">
    Under **Sorting type**, choose **Closed** (the default) or **Open**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add categories">
    For a closed sort, add at least 2 **Categories**. For an open sort, you can add **Starting categories (optional)** that participants can use alongside their own. You can add up to 30 categories of up to 120 characters each, and drag them to reorder.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add cards">
    Click **Add cards** to open the **Cards** window. Type a card name (up to 120 characters), then press Enter or click **Add new card** for the next one. Drag cards to reorder them. You can add up to 100 cards. Click **Done** to save; it stays disabled until at least one card has a name.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Card settings

The **Cards** window has three settings:

- **Add tooltip descriptions.** Add a short description to each card (up to 300 characters). Participants see it as a tooltip.
- **Add images.** Attach an image to each card (PNG, JPEG, or WebP, up to 5 MB).
- **Hide card names.** Show only the image to participants. Available when **Add images** is on.

To change cards later, click **Edit cards**.

## What participants see

**Human participants** sort by dragging cards from the **Cards** list into categories. Unless you wrote your own instructions, they see:

- **Closed sort:** "Drag each card into the category where you think it belongs."
- **Open sort:** "Drag cards into groups that make sense to you, and name each group." They can drop a card on the new-category area or click **Create category** to make a new group, then rename or remove the groups they created.

**Finish task** becomes available once at least one card is sorted. Participants don't have to sort every card. The card sort appears in the language you choose for your [test link](/test-links), in any of the 9 participant languages.

**AI participants** sort every card based on their profile: their background, vocabulary, and habits. They see your instructions, descriptions, and images, just like human participants.
