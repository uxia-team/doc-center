In an **unmoderated test**, participants complete your test on their own, without a moderator. You build the test from [blocks](/build-your-test) — tasks, surveys, card sorts, and accessibility checks — and run it with **AI participants**, **human participants**, or both.

## How an unmoderated test works

Every unmoderated test moves through three phases, shown at the top of the test as **Build › Recruit › Analyze**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Build">
    Add the blocks your test needs and configure each one. A single test can combine, for example, a [Task block](/task-block) on your checkout flow, a [Survey block](/survey-block) with follow-up questions, and an [Accessibility check](/accessibility-block) of your key screens. See [Build your test](/build-your-test).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recruit">
    Choose who takes the test: [AI participants](/ai-participants) from your audiences, your own participants through [test links](/test-links), or a recruited [human panel](/human-panel). You can combine them. See [Recruit participants](/recruit-participants).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Analyze">
    Results stream in while the test runs. Review each block's results, switch between AI and human participants, ask questions about your results with [Ask AI](/ask-ai), and share or export them. See [Analyze results](/analyze-results).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## AI participants, humans, or both

AI participants give you fast, repeatable, low-cost feedback — most tests finish within minutes. Human participants add real-world nuance and validation. The best research teams know when to use each, and when to combine them.

### When AI participants shine

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="When speed matters">
    Validate ideas in minutes instead of days. Ideal for rapid design iterations, fix → test → improve cycles, early prototype validation, sprint reviews, and feature validation. AI participants are best when you need quick, directional insight to move forward with confidence rather than a final research conclusion.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When resources are limited">
    No incentives, scheduling, or moderation. Ideal for agencies managing multiple clients, teams without dedicated researchers, and budget-sensitive teams that want frequent validation. Once an audience is defined, it's available on demand.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When your audience is hard to recruit">
    Healthcare professionals, financial experts, developers, and niche B2B audiences are expensive and slow to recruit. An [audience](/audiences/overview) built with the right profile lets you test with a representation of them whenever you need.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Before investing in human testing">
    Catch obvious usability issues first, so your human sessions can focus on deeper questions — emotional reactions, nuanced decisions, edge cases. AI participants work as a preparation layer: you arrive at human testing with a cleaner prototype and sharper hypotheses.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When you want to test different scenarios">
    Evaluate the same experience under different contexts by changing the [scenario](/test-scenario) — for example, a checkout completed in a hurry, as a gift, or on behalf of someone else. People can't easily step outside their own context; AI participants can.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When you're getting started with usability testing">
    Launch a test in minutes, improve, and test again. The short feedback loop helps teams without research experience build a testing habit.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When you want continuous validation">
    Test earlier, test smaller changes, and test more often — ideal for weekly releases, continuous discovery, CRO, and design critiques.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When your team needs evidence to decide">
    Replace internal debate with a structured view of friction points, expectations, and improvement opportunities, so you can prioritize and align faster.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### When to add human participants

Add human participants when you need to validate high-stakes decisions, capture emotional reactions, or hear from your actual customers. Because AI and human participants take the **same test**, you can combine them in one study: AI participants surface issues within minutes, and real people you invite with [test links](/test-links) validate them with real behavior. You review both in the same results.

<Tip>
  Once AI participants launch, the test's blocks are locked. To test an improved version of your design, create a new test.
</Tip>

<Note>
  **Where AI participants aren't the right fit:** experiences carried by animation, video, or motion. AI participants don't perceive motion the way a person does — if that's the core of what you're validating, test with human participants.
</Note>
