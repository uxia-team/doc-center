Questions are the core of a [Survey block](/survey-block). The way you phrase and structure them shapes everything you get back. You can mix types in one survey: closed questions for signal you can count, open ones for depth.

## Question basics

Click **Add question** at the bottom of a Survey block and pick a type. Every question, whatever its type, has:

- **Question.** The prompt participants answer. Every question is required.
- **Context** *(optional)*. Click **+ Add more context** to open **Add more context**. Add background, clarify terminology, or give instructions that apply only to this question. You can also attach up to 5 **Context images** (PNG, JPEG, or WebP, up to 5 MB each) when you want participants to react to a concept, screen, or design. Context images are reference material, not answer options.

Each question card also has:

- A **type dropdown** next to the question number. Switching type keeps your question text and context.
- A **drag handle** to reorder questions.
- A **…** menu with **Move up**, **Move down**, **Duplicate**, and **Delete**.

Every answer comes with a detailed explanation, so a rating or a choice arrives with the *why* attached.

<Tip>
  Lead with broad, open questions and narrow toward specifics later. Asking for a first impression before you show defined options keeps early answers from being anchored by your choices.
</Tip>

## The five question types

### Open-ended

A free-text answer. This is where you uncover motivations, unexpected reactions, and the reasoning behind behavior, in participants' own words. In the results, answers are grouped into recurring topics.

### Multiple choice

Participants choose from a list of options you define.

- Add **2 to 10 options**. Drag options to reorder them, and use each option's **…** menu to duplicate or delete it.
- Choose **Single selection** (one answer) or **Multiple selection** (several answers).
- Attach an **image to each option** (PNG, JPEG, or WebP, up to 5 MB) when participants should compare visuals, such as two logo directions or three packaging designs. An option can be an image alone.

### Rating scale

Quantify sentiment or satisfaction. Choose a **Scale** of **1-5**, **1-7**, or **1-10**, then fill in the **Low label** and **High label** (for example, "Very difficult" and "Very easy"). Both labels are required, so participants and your readers share the same anchors.

### Ranking

Participants put a set of items in order. Add **2 to 10 items**, then fill in the **Low label** and **High label** (for example, "Least important" and "Most important"). Both labels are required. Ranking is useful for forcing trade-offs between options that would each score well on their own.

### Budget allocation

Participants split **100 points** across **2 to 10 items** by importance. Because the points are limited, this shows relative priority more honestly than rating each item on its own: participants have to decide what matters most.

<Note>
  Ranking and budget allocation both measure relative preference, but they answer different questions. Ranking gives you a strict order. Budget allocation shows you *how much* more one item matters than another.
</Note>

<Note>
  Older tests can include **Yes / No** questions. You can no longer add them; use a **Multiple choice** question with two options instead.
</Note>

## Choosing the right mix

| You want to know… | Use |
|---|---|
| The reasoning behind a response | Open-ended |
| Which option wins, or a clear yes or no | Multiple choice |
| How strongly participants feel | Rating scale |
| The order of preference | Ranking |
| Where priorities really sit | Budget allocation |

## Limits and credits

- **Questions per test:** 30 across all Survey blocks on paid plans, 5 on the Free plan.
- **Credits:** 1 credit per question per AI participant.
