An **audience** defines who your AI participants are. It's a reusable set of criteria that Uxia turns into a panel of AI participants: synthetic people who navigate, react, and think aloud the way a real person matching that audience would. You get usable feedback in minutes, without recruiting or scheduling anyone. Build an audience once from **AI Audience** in the sidebar or while choosing participants in the Recruit phase, then reuse it across as many tests as you like.

Audiences apply to AI participants only. To test with real people, invite your own participants with a [test link](/test-links) or request a [human panel](/human-panel). You can combine AI and human participants in the same test.

## The three layers of an audience

Every Uxia audience is built on three layers that stack into a complete, believable person.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Sociodemographic">
    The foundation — age, gender, languages, country and nationality, education, employment, industry, role, income, and more.

    You set only what matters to you; whatever you leave blank, Uxia completes using publicly available data sources so each AI participant stays statistically representative of the population they belong to, rather than filled with random values.

    On an AI participant's profile, the fields you set are styled differently from the ones Uxia filled in. Hover a field to see whether it was auto-populated.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Psychological">
    Every AI participant carries a personality based on the **OCEAN model** (the Big Five) — Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism — one of the most established frameworks in psychology for describing personality and predicting behavior.

    It's shown as a radar chart and shapes how an AI participant approaches tasks, reacts to friction, and phrases feedback.

    This is where you tune the _temperament_ of your panel: test with users who are easily frustrated or highly patient, more or less empathetic, cautious or exploratory.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Enrichment">
    An optional custom layer that uses your own data — interviews, personas, research — to generate AI participants with far higher fidelity to your real users. See [Audience Enrichment](/audiences/enrichment).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## What makes Uxia's AI participants different

Uxia's AI participants aren't generic language-model output. Each is grounded in proprietary behavioral and psychographic datasets that model how people with specific backgrounds and motivations actually use digital products. So a 22-year-old student with low tech literacy doesn't just _say_ different things than a 45-year-old IT manager — they _behave_ differently, hesitating in different places, trusting different signals, and reading UI through different mental models.

During a test, each AI participant works through your product while **thinking aloud** — narrating what they expect, calling out confusion or surprise, and explaining why they did or didn't click something. That narration is captured as a full transcript, giving you the reasoning behind every interaction rather than just the outcome.

## Example AI participant profile

To make this concrete, here's what an AI participant looks like in practice:

> **Maya R.** — 34 years old, Marketing Manager, London, UK
>
> Maya uses SaaS tools daily across project management, CRM, and analytics platforms. She has moderate tech literacy — comfortable with mainstream software but unlikely to explore settings menus or read documentation. She moves quickly through interfaces and relies heavily on familiar visual patterns. Maya is cautious about sharing personal or company data and will abandon a flow if she encounters an unclear permissions prompt. She responds well to social proof and recognizable brand signals.

When Maya runs your test, her profile shapes where she pauses, what she reads carefully, and what she skips entirely — producing a session that reflects how a real user matching her profile would experience your product.
