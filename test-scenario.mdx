The **Scenario** field is a short backstory for the task. Where the [Task](/mission) says _what_ participants should do, the scenario says _what's happening in their life_ that leads them to do it. It helps participants imagine a realistic situation, so they behave like real users rather than task-followers.

The scenario is required in every Task block and takes up to 500 characters.

## What a good scenario does

A scenario sets the stage without prescribing actions. For example:

> "You've just moved to another country and want to open a bank account to receive your salary."
>
> "You're evaluating project management tools for a team of five and have just landed on this product's homepage."

Neither one says what to click. They establish motivation and mindset, which produces more authentic reactions, richer think-aloud comments, and more realistic concerns.

A good scenario:

- Uses the second person and present tense: "You are…", "You have…", "You want…".
- Sets up a believable moment that gives a reason to act right now.
- Doesn't restate the task.

<Tip>
  Keep the scenario short and grounded in a believable situation. One or two sentences are enough. You don't need a full persona here, since that's what your [audience](/audiences/overview) is for.
</Tip>

Like the task, the scenario has a **Tips** button that suggests rewrites. See [Refine it with Tips](/mission#refine-it-with-tips).

## How the fields fit together

<Warning>
  **Differentiate between Task, Scenario, Stop condition, and Audience**

  The **Task** is what participants do (the "what"). The **Audience** is who performs it (the "who"). The **Stop condition** is when the session ends (the "until when"). The **Scenario** is the situational context (the "why", or the circumstances in which the task is performed).

  For example:

  **Task**: Purchase a 1-hour travel ticket in this subway app.

  **Scenario**: You have just landed in New York for the first time for a 10-day summer holiday, and you need a way to get around the city using the subway.

  **Stop condition**: Stop when you reach the credit card information screen.

  **Audience**: Male or female, 25–40 years old, born in the UK, full-time employed.
</Warning>

A media prototype task has no stop condition: the run ends at the final frame of the prototype.
